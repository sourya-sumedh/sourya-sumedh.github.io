PROGRAM SCALING · CONTRACT GOVERNANCE · CROSS-FUNCTIONAL LEADERSHIP

Scaled transformation of Enhanced Software Evergreen Services

Transformed a fragmented subscription-delivery program into a standardized, contract-driven operating model for continuous software updates.

Role
Program Manager, North American Professional Services
Business model
3-5 year continuous software subscription lifecycle
Impact
Scaled delivery capacity by 50% and safeguarded $3M in contract value

Challenge

No centralized contract tracking, ad-hoc implementation practices, unclear handoffs, and high risk of unbilled overages.

Action

Built a governed delivery framework with intake gates, parallel workstreams, consumption tracking, SOPs, and escalation paths.

Result

Created a scalable subscription program with clearer ownership, faster deployments, and stronger margin protection.

Read the detailed case study

Situation

The organization moved from standalone project delivery to a continuous software update model covering operating systems, applications, branding, and marketing services. Delivery needed to respect strict contractual guardrails, including limited major upgrade builds, annual refreshes, and predefined update entitlements.

Operating Model

I standardized the lifecycle into five phases: intake and trigger, kick-off and scope validation, execution, validation and deployment, and closure and tracking. A build-versus-refresh gate clarified when a request was a simple update versus a resource-intensive core rebuild.

Execution Architecture

To remove linear bottlenecks, I introduced parallel execution across CXM updates, branding updates, shared properties, and copy-file packages. Each stream followed the same repeatable flow, giving engineering teams consistency without slowing them down.

Governance

I launched a central QuickBase tracker for eligibility, annual usage, and consumed upgrade credits. Scope thresholds halted out-of-contract work and redirected accounts to Sales when add-on or upsell handling was required.

Enablement

Standard kick-off kits, requirement triage workflows, KPI dashboards, SOP flowcharts, and walkthrough documentation helped Professional Services, Managed Services, technical SMEs, Sales, and customers work from the same source of truth.

Business Impact

The program scaled without added headcount, eliminated unbilled overages on core platform builds, improved update predictability, reduced handover friction, and gave customers a clearer subscription experience.

Standardizing a Delivery Framework for a Software Lifecycle Management Program

Designed and institutionalized a standardized delivery framework for a software lifecycle management program supporting 170+ customer agreements, replacing an informal, tribal-knowledge approach with repeatable process, governance, and reporting.

Role
Project Manager, Software Lifecycle Management Program
Portfolio
170+ contracted customer agreements, $2.5M+ subscription value
Impact
Scaled from pilot activities into a fully governed, cross-team operating model

Challenge

No standardized process, governance, or reporting for a fast-growing program; delivery depended on individual experience rather than documented procedure.

Action

Built an end-to-end delivery process with WBS, RACI, kickoff templates, training content, and standardized tracking and reporting; ran enablement sessions across PM teams.

Result

Repeatable operating model that reduced new-PM onboarding time, improved portfolio visibility, and coordinated delivery across Professional Services, Managed Services, Sales, and Customer Success.

Read the detailed case study

Situation

A newly introduced software lifecycle management program had grown to 170+ contracted customer agreements without a standardized way to run it. Project initiation, scope assessment, stakeholder engagement, testing, and closure all varied by individual PM, making onboarding slow and portfolio-level reporting unreliable.

Approach

I designed a standardized end-to-end delivery process and built the supporting assets: process documentation, flowcharts, work breakdown structures, a RACI chart, kickoff and customer-communication templates, training materials, FAQs, and cheat sheets. I updated the PMIS for consistent data capture and led training and enablement sessions to establish a shared understanding of delivery expectations across project management teams.

Governance & Reporting

I implemented standardized tracking and reporting to give visibility into active, completed, on-hold, and upcoming projects, and consolidated portfolio data across all 170+ agreements so leadership could see refresh status, upcoming demand, and execution priorities. This also supported governance work that clarified ownership, accountability, and handoff points across participating teams.

Business Impact

Over 12 months, the program moved from an informal delivery approach to a scalable, repeatable operating model. I provided operational oversight for 30+ completed software refresh activities that became baseline data for planning and governance decisions, and the reusable framework cut onboarding time for new project managers by replacing tribal knowledge with documented procedure.

Long-Term Value

The framework became the foundation for ongoing program execution and future process improvement, scaling the service from a Professional Services delivery activity into a coordinated mechanism spanning Professional Services, Managed Services, Sales, and Customer Success.

STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT · CONFLICT RESOLUTION · COMMUNICATION

Managing Difficult Conversations in Project Delivery

Developed a consistent approach to de-escalating tense conversations among customers, vendors, and delivery teams during slipping deadlines and technical setbacks, keeping discussions focused on solutions rather than blame.

Role
Project / Program Manager
Context
Cross-functional delivery across customers, vendors, and internal teams
Impact
Repeatable de-escalation approach that preserved stakeholder trust under pressure

Challenge

Deadline slippage and complex technical issues repeatedly turned status conversations into tense exchanges among customers, vendors, and delivery teams, putting trust at risk.

Action

Applied a consistent set of principles: acknowledge pressure, understand business impact, facilitate rather than referee technical debate, listen before responding, and follow up after the meeting.

Result

Turned difficult conversations into productive ones, rebuilding customer confidence through visible ownership, transparency, and clear next steps.

Read the detailed case study

Situation

Successful delivery is not only a technical problem — it is also about managing people, expectations, and emotions. As deadlines slipped and complex issues surfaced, conversations among customers, vendors, and delivery teams became tense, and the challenge shifted from technical to relational: keeping discussions productive while trust was under strain.

Approach

I slowed emotionally charged conversations down rather than jumping straight to solutions, acknowledging concerns to create space for productive discussion. With stakeholders, I focused on understanding the business impact they were facing — a missed commitment, a go-live at risk — and paired that with transparency about what was being done and what came next.

Facilitating Technical Discussions

When tension rose among technical experts, I intervened early with internal team members, redirecting or taking detailed debate offline. With vendor partners, I reset the room around the shared objective. With frustrated customers, I avoided becoming defensive, acknowledged the concern, and committed to clear next steps — ownership and transparency mattered more than perfection.

Listening & Follow-Through

Before responding, I summarized what I had heard to confirm understanding and reduce defensiveness, and kept discussion focused on the problem rather than the person. De-escalation didn't end when the meeting did — documented action items, status updates, and individual check-ins reinforced accountability afterward.

Outcome

This approach consistently turned strained conversations back toward collaboration. Complex technical problems could usually be solved; the harder risk was loss of trust, and a calm, structured response to pressure protected that trust across customer, vendor, and internal relationships.

ISSUE MANAGEMENT · CROSS-FUNCTIONAL GOVERNANCE · REPORTING

Building a Structured Support-Ticket Governance Process

Introduced a structured support-issue tracking and escalation process across Support, Engineering, and Professional Services, replacing scattered ServiceNow data with centralized visibility and accountability.

Role
Project Manager (QA background), Professional Services
Context
Multiple customer engagements tracked in ServiceNow across Support, Engineering, and PS
Impact
Centralized issue visibility and escalation governance across three teams

Challenge

No high-level visibility into open support cases, inconsistent follow-up on aging tickets, and no standardized reporting, forcing PMs to manually gather status from multiple systems.

Action

Defined process gaps and reporting requirements, built recurring severity-based incident reports, introduced review and escalation cycles, and facilitated joint reviews across delivery, support, and engineering.

Result

Turned support tracking from a reactive activity into a structured operational process with clearer ownership, faster escalation, and more accurate stakeholder communication.

Read the detailed case study

Situation

As production and implementation-related support issues grew across multiple customer engagements, project teams had no consistent way to track issue status, ownership, escalation paths, or progress toward resolution. Information was scattered across systems, making it hard for PMs to give accurate status updates or spot aging issues, and there was no combined ownership model between Support, Engineering, and Professional Services for critical, multi-customer issues.

Process Definition

I identified gaps in the existing support tracking workflow, contributed requirements for improved visibility into incident status and ownership, and recommended reporting metrics that gave better insight into ticket aging, assignment history, and resolution progress.

Reporting Framework

I created recurring incident review reports consolidating data from multiple tracking systems, categorized tickets by severity and business impact, and established a review mechanism that validated open incidents and confirmed resolved ones — preventing resolved issues from reappearing in future reports.

Governance & Escalation

I introduced regular review cycles for high-priority and aging issues, worked with support and engineering stakeholders to improve visibility into related development work, and surfaced issues needing management escalation to existing governance forums.

Business Impact

The initiative improved visibility into issue status across teams, reduced the effort required to collect and validate support updates, increased accountability for ticket ownership, and improved escalation of critical and aging incidents — enabling PMs to give more accurate stakeholder and project status communications.
